Part three · The model
15

The five things.

They work at team level, whatever the team runs: Windows and network one year, Terraform and AWS the next.
01Reach
Grow ↑

How widely the thing is actually used. It only counts when people could have said no.

Watch for shadow kit springing up around anything nobody can opt out of. That’s reach leaking away, and it’s the most honest signal you’ll get.

02Depththe real prize
Grow ↑

How much more a team can do because you exist. A production-ready service used to take six weeks and favours from three teams. Now it takes an afternoon.

The win isn’t the six weeks. Starting something now costs almost nothing, so people try ten times as many ideas.

03Cognitive load
Shrink ↓

The thinking you force on the people you serve: every step, form and ticket they need just to use your stuff. Measure the friction, not the feelings.

Sweller named it in 1988. Team Topologies (2019) made it a team-design rule. The 2023 DevEx paper — ACM Queue; Forsgren (Accelerate, Microsoft Research), Storey (University of Victoria), Noda & Greiler (DX) — made it one of its three dimensions, with feedback loops and flow state.

04Toil
Shrink ↓

The manual grind on your own side. The same steps, by hand, again.

Google’s SRE rule of thumb: keep it under half the team’s time.

05Reliability
The multiplier

Whether people can trust it. SLOs and error budgets, scored nought to one. If trust goes, everything else drags towards zero.

DORA 2024: teams on an internal platform were more productive but 14% less stable on change. Platforms lift the ceiling and can crack the floor.

It can go negative

If load and toil outweigh reach times depth, the platform is a net drain. Never become that.

Keep them in mind — don't chase them

A lens, not a target.

When a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure.

GOODHART'S LAW
DORA say the same of their own five

Apply them per service, to improve your own team over time. Not to compete, not to compare teams.

This one is for every day

Ask it of every ticket and every card on the board, not just the monitoring dashboard.

A decision lens, not a validated instrument · never a stick Still no validated, platform-specific measure. The field’s first systematic review (Anjum, 2026) lists one as its top gap.

The IDP ladder.

Six shapes, thinnest first — every one counts as a platform (CNCF).
06 Invisible platform Ask: nothing
The default is paved. You notice it when it's gone.
05 Platform as product Ask: a product
Roadmap, PM, users. DORA's “platform” starts here.
04 Self-service Ask: a button
Portal or API. No human in the loop.
03 Pipelines & policy Ask: a pipeline
Change as code, checked, applied on green.
02 Templates & modules Ask: a template
Golden paths and scaffolds.
01 Docs & runbooks Ask: a person
How to use the services you've already got.
Rungs 1–3: where most estates live — and do real leverage

Every rung costs more to run. The top is not the goal.

Climb only when this rung keeps failing the test — a minimum viable platform (DORA).

Part four · In practice
18

Two everyday examples.

Bad and good, on the five measures.
Reach (them) Depth (them) Load (us) Toil (us) Floor
Example 1 · A team needs a new cloud account.
Bad: build it by hand when asked This request only They wait on us We remember the steps Same steps next time Depends on us
Good: describe the account as code, the pipeline builds it Anyone asks the same way Built without waiting on a person The code remembers None next time Reviewed first
Example 2 · Teams keep asking how to get access to a system.
Bad: answer in chat each time Whoever asked Answered, eventually We hold the answer Same answer again Depends on who answers
Good: one-page guide, linked from the service desk Everyone who looks Answered without us Written down once None Same answer every time

Part five · The takeaway
19

When anyone asks you for something.

We work in response to everyone else: product, security, finance, audit, the business. The request is reactive. The answer needn’t be.
Reach Who else is going to need this? If the answer is anyone, build it once for all of them, not once for this ticket.
Depth What will it let them do that they couldn’t before? That’s the prize, and it’s bigger than the ticket.
Load How much thinking am I handing them? Every step, form and ticket they need just to use it. Fewer.
Toil What’s left for us to do by hand next time? If it’s the same thing again, automate it or write it down.
Floor Does it stay trustworthy? Reviewed, repeatable, the same every time. If not, none of the rest counts.
Where each part comes from Mostly Fournier & Nowland. The model puts it in one place you can hold in your head.
Reach × Depth Leverage: a few engineers reducing the work of the many, by making teams more capable and removing duplicated work. Adoption only counts with a choice. F&N
Load Cognitive load is why platform teams exist. Team Topologies · DevEx 2023
Toil Plan for operational load; keep lights-on work under 40%. Toil under half your time. F&N · SRE
Floor A platform nobody trusts is worth nothing. Reliability is operational performance, measured apart from delivery. F&N · DORA
