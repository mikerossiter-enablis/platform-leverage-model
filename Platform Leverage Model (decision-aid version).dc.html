The five forces
06
Force 01 · Grow it

Leverage.

A platform earns its place as a force multiplier: a small team does a job once so dozens of others don't. Turn the repetitive graft — provisioning, pipelines, environments — into something reusable. Build the thinnest viable platform: as little as possible, as well as possible.

Grounded in
Team Topologies · Wardley Mapping · Theory of Constraints
1 → many
Fan-out · teams per engineer
What to measure
Your fan-out — teams or workloads per engineer — and whether the teams you serve are improving: recovering faster, shipping sooner.
What it replaces
Counting how often you ship. Your value is the multiplier effect, not your own deploy count.

The five forces
07
Force 02 · Grow it · Earned

Adoption.

A good platform is one people choose, not one they're forced onto. If you have to mandate it, it probably isn't good enough yet. For services no one can opt out of — network, DNS, the service desk — watch the inverse: people working around you, or standing up shadow kit. That's your warning light.

Grounded in
Platform-as-a-product · DORA
Pull > push
Chosen, not mandated
What to measure
Voluntary use — the share of eligible work that comes to you without anyone being forced. If it's mandated, the number tells you nothing.
What it replaces
Treating forced usage as proof of value.

The five forces
08
Force 03 · Shrink it · On those you serve

Cognitive load.

Take weight off the people who depend on you — fewer things to understand, fewer hoops to jump through. You can't measure load directly, so measure the friction that stands in for it.

Grounded in
Team Topologies · Cognitive load theory
days → minutes
Time to get what they need
What to measure
Proxies from your own systems: time to get a server, a firewall change, a reset; steps to finish a common task; how much runs with no human in the loop.
What it replaces
The developer-experience questionnaire. Measure friction from the system, not from self-reports.

09
Force 04 · Shrink it · On your own team

Toil.

The manual, repetitive work that keeps the lights on but can't be reused and grows with the systems — restarts, hand-patching, re-keying the same request. Above half your team's time and you never get the room to automate it away, so it snowballs.

Grounded in
Google SRE
< 50%
Of team time on toil · ~⅓ is healthy
What to measure
Share of team time on toil vs. real engineering. Aim under 50%; healthy teams sit near a third. Pull it from time-tracking, tickets and on-call logs.
What it replaces
Nothing — this is your own health measure, and it's the one that protects the others.

The five forces
10
Force 05 · The floor you never breach

Reliability.

Set clear targets for the services people depend on — an SLO, say 99.9% available — and track how fast you're spending your error budget. Aiming for 100% is the wrong target: that small budget is exactly what lets you change and improve.

Grounded in
Google SRE · DORA
99.9%
SLO · and the error budget it buys
What to measure
Whether you're hitting targets, and how fast you're burning error budget. Out of budget? Improvement work pauses until you're back inside it.
What it replaces
Chasing a speed number. For infra, reliability is the anchor.

Be honest about what this is
13

What this isn't.

Not a proven instrument

A way of thinking, not a peer-reviewed model. There's still no validated platform-specific instrument in the research (Anjum, 2026).

Load is only a proxy

Cognitive load is the weak spot: the measures capture friction, which only stands in for load. A direction of travel, not a verdict.

Fan-out is home-grown

"Fan-out" is a figure put together here for leverage, not an established metric. Adapt it to your world.

A mirror, not a stick

Use all of this to improve yourselves — not as a stick to beat teams with.
